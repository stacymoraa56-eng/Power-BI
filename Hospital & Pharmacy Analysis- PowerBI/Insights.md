# Hospital & Pharmacy Data Analysis: Written Insights

## Introduction

This analysis uses Power BI to investigate patient visits, disease patterns, pharmacy revenue, medication consumption, and hospital length of stay across the available hospital dataset. The objective is to identify meaningful patterns that can help hospital management understand operational performance and areas requiring further investigation.

## 1. Disease Patterns Across Counties

The analysis identified flu, typhoid, and diabetes among the most frequently recorded diagnoses across the counties represented in the dataset. Understanding the distribution of these diagnoses can help management investigate differences in healthcare demand across counties. However, these findings describe the records analyzed and should not be interpreted as estimates of disease prevalence in the wider population.

## 2. Patient Visits and Pharmacy Revenue

The comparison between patient visits and pharmacy revenue revealed a positive pattern in the observed data. Uasin Gishu recorded the highest patient visits and pharmacy revenue during the analyzed period, while the county with the fewest visits also recorded the lowest revenue.

This observation suggests that higher patient volumes may be associated with higher pharmacy revenue. However, patient visits alone may not explain revenue differences. Medication prices, prescription patterns, patient demographics, and the types of conditions treated may also contribute. Further analysis would be required to determine the strength and consistency of the relationship.

## 3. Pharmacy Costs by Department

Inpatient recorded the highest pharmacy cost at 95K, followed by Emergency at 94K and Outpatient at 87K. Inpatient and Emergency were therefore the largest contributors to the reported departmental pharmacy costs.

Management could investigate the factors driving these expenditures, including medication utilization, patient volumes, and treatment requirements. Comparing expenditure per patient or per visit could provide additional insight into departmental spending patterns.

## 4. Medication Consumption Across Age Groups

The age-group analysis indicated that elderly patients accounted for the highest total medication consumption, with consumption generally decreasing among younger groups. This pattern highlights the importance of examining medication demand across patient demographics.

However, total consumption does not necessarily indicate that individual elderly patients consume more medication than younger patients. Differences in patient numbers and the metric used to measure consumption should be considered when interpreting these findings.

## 5. Diagnosis, Pharmacy Spending, and Length of Stay

The analysis revealed varying relationships between diagnoses, pharmacy spending, and average hospital length of stay. Typhoid was associated with the highest pharmacy spending and a high average length of stay. Malaria showed comparatively lower spending and shorter stays, while flu was associated with a longer hospital stay and average spending.

These observations suggest that the relationship between hospital length of stay and pharmacy expenditure differs across diagnoses. There is no single consistent pattern across all the conditions examined.

## Conclusion and Recommendations

The analysis provides an overview of disease patterns, patient activity, medication consumption, and pharmacy expenditure across the available dataset.

Management could use these findings to investigate county-level demand, monitor pharmacy revenue, review departmental expenditure, and better understand medication utilization across patient groups.

Further analysis should prioritize revenue per visit, medication consumption per patient, and the factors associated with hospital length of stay. These measures could provide deeper insights into operational performance and support more informed resource planning.

The findings should be interpreted within the limitations of the available data. They identify patterns and associations but do not independently establish causation or clinical effectiveness.
